Figure: Screenshot of Genomic Region in Chromosome 3 of N. whitei

This screenshot shows a section of chromosome 3 from Nothobranchius whitei using the IGV genome browser. I included different types of data tracks to highlight what’s happening in this part of the genome. At the top, the purple arc represents a chromatin loop. This suggests that two distant regions of DNA might physically come close together in the cell. That could mean they interact in some way. For example, one region might help control gene activity in the other.
The red peaks show RNA-seq data. This reflects gene expression under two different conditions: diapause and larva. Taller peaks mean that more RNA is being made from that region. That means those genes are more active. The blue peaks represent ATAC-seq data. These show open regions in the DNA. These are spots where the DNA isn’t tightly packed and might be accessible to proteins that control gene expression. When these peaks fall outside of gene areas, they might be important regulatory regions even though they don’t code for proteins. At the bottom, the green boxes represent the locations of genes. Seeing open chromatin and RNA expression in this area, along with a DNA loop, suggests that these features could be working together to regulate gene activity.
